# Gratuity Calculation UAE: How to Calculate Your End-of-Service Pay

If you've worked for a private-sector employer in the UAE for at least a year, you're owed a lump sum when you leave. It's called end-of-service gratuity, and the formula is set by law. You can check it yourself with your payslip and a calculator.

The gratuity calculation in the UAE comes down to three numbers: your basic salary, your length of service, and a daily rate. Most mistakes happen because people use their full package instead of their basic salary, or because they still believe resigning cuts their gratuity. Under the current law, it doesn't.

This guide walks through the rules, the four steps, and six worked examples, including resignation, a part year, unpaid leave and the two-year cap.

## The rules behind UAE gratuity

[UAE labour law](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector) (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, Article 51) sets out end-of-service gratuity for full-time private-sector employees:

- You must complete at least one year of continuous service. Less than a year means no gratuity.
- You get 21 days of basic salary for each of your first five years.
- You get 30 days of basic salary for each year after the fifth.
- Part years count. Once you've passed one year, the extra months are paid in proportion.
- Days of unpaid leave or unpaid absence are taken out of your service.
- The total can't be more than two years' wage.
- It's based on your last basic salary, not your package.

Your employer then has 14 days from the end of your contract to pay your final dues, under Article 53.

### What counts as basic salary

Basic salary is the figure on your contract or payslip labelled "basic". It doesn't include housing, transport, phone or other allowances, commission, or bonuses.

This matters a lot in the UAE, where basic is often half or less of the total package. Someone on AED 20,000 a month might have a basic of AED 9,000. Their gratuity is worked out on AED 9,000.

### Who isn't covered

Some people aren't paid gratuity under these rules:

- UAE and GCC nationals are generally covered by a pension scheme instead.
- Employees in the DIFC are paid through the DEWS savings plan, covered below.
- Some mainland employers have moved staff to the voluntary savings scheme, also covered below.
- Part-time workers are covered, but their gratuity is worked out in proportion to their hours.

## How to calculate gratuity in the UAE in 4 steps

Most employers and online calculators use the same four steps.

**Step 1: Find your daily basic wage.** Divide your monthly basic salary by 30.

**Step 2: Work out your service.** Count from your start date to your last working day. Take out any unpaid leave days.

**Step 3: Count your gratuity days.** Multiply your first five years by 21. Multiply any years after that by 30. Add the two.

**Step 4: Multiply.** Gratuity days × daily basic wage = your gratuity. Then check it against the two-year cap.

As a formula:

> Gratuity = (basic ÷ 30) × [(21 × years up to 5) + (30 × years after 5)]

### ÷30 or ×12÷365?

Most calculators divide your monthly basic by 30. Some payroll teams and courts use an annual basis instead: monthly basic × 12 ÷ 365. That gives a slightly lower daily rate.

On a basic of AED 12,000, ÷30 gives AED 400 a day and ×12÷365 gives about AED 394.52. Over seven years of service, that's a difference of about AED 900. If your settlement looks a little lower than you expected, this is often why. It's worth asking HR which method they used.

## Gratuity calculation UAE examples

All six examples use the ÷30 method.

### Example 1: three years' service

Basic salary: AED 6,000. Service: exactly three years.

- Daily wage: 6,000 ÷ 30 = AED 200
- Gratuity days: 3 × 21 = 63
- Gratuity: 63 × 200 = **AED 12,600**

### Example 2: seven years' service

Basic salary: AED 12,000. Service: seven years.

- Daily wage: 12,000 ÷ 30 = AED 400
- First five years: 5 × 21 = 105 days
- Next two years: 2 × 30 = 60 days
- Total: 165 days × 400 = **AED 66,000**

### Example 3: a part year

Basic salary: AED 9,000. Service: two years and six months.

- Daily wage: 9,000 ÷ 30 = AED 300
- Gratuity days: 2.5 × 21 = 52.5
- Gratuity: 52.5 × 300 = **AED 15,750**

If this person had left at 11 months instead, they'd get nothing. The one-year minimum is a hard line.

### Example 4: unpaid leave

Basic salary: AED 10,000. Service: four years, including 60 days of unpaid leave.

- Daily wage: 10,000 ÷ 30 = about AED 333.33
- Service after removing unpaid leave: 4 − (60 ÷ 365) = about 3.84 years
- Gratuity days: 3.84 × 21 = about 80.5
- Gratuity: 80.5 × 333.33 = about **AED 26,850**

Without the unpaid leave, it would have been AED 28,000. Two months off cost about AED 1,150 in gratuity. Paid annual leave still counts as service.

### Example 5: resignation

Basic salary: AED 8,000. Service: four years. The employee resigns.

- Daily wage: 8,000 ÷ 30 = about AED 266.67
- Gratuity days: 4 × 21 = 84
- Gratuity: 84 × 266.67 = **AED 22,400**

That's the full amount. See the next section for why.

### Example 6: when the two-year cap applies

Basic salary: AED 15,000. Service: 30 years.

- Daily wage: 15,000 ÷ 30 = AED 500
- Gratuity days: (5 × 21) + (25 × 30) = 855
- Uncapped gratuity: 855 × 500 = AED 427,500
- Cap: two years' basic = 24 × 15,000 = **AED 360,000**

The cap only bites after about 25 years with the same employer, so most people won't reach it.

## Gratuity on resignation in the UAE

This is the most common myth. Under the old labour law, people on unlimited contracts who resigned early got only a third or two-thirds of their gratuity. Many older articles and calculators still apply that cut.

The current law took effect on 2 February 2022 and removed it. If you resign after at least one year, you get the same gratuity as someone whose contract was ended by the employer.

There is one exception worth checking. If part of your service was on an old unlimited contract before February 2022, that earlier period may be calculated under the rules that applied then. If you're in that position, ask HR to show you the split, or check with MOHRE.

Resigning still affects other things, though. You need to serve your notice period. And if you resign, you won't be able to claim unemployment insurance. Our [emergency fund guide](/blog/emergency-fund-uae) explains how ILOE and gratuity fit together.

## DIFC and the voluntary savings scheme

Not everyone gets a lump sum calculated at the end.

If you work in the DIFC, you're covered by DEWS (DIFC Employee Workplace Savings). Instead of paying gratuity when you leave, your employer pays into a savings plan each month: 5.83% of your basic salary for your first five years, and 8.33% after that. That's the same as 21 and 30 days a year, paid in advance. The money is invested, so the final amount can be higher or lower than gratuity would have been.

On the mainland, some employers have moved staff to the voluntary alternative end-of-service scheme, set up under [Cabinet Resolution No. 96 of 2023](https://mohre.gov.ae/en/media-center/news/1/11/2023/voluntary-alternative-end-of-service-benefits-scheme-goes-into-effect-by-cabinet-resolution-aiming-t). It works in a similar way: the employer pays monthly contributions into an approved fund at the same 5.83% and 8.33% rates, and you can add your own contributions on top.

If you're not sure which applies to you, check your contract or ask HR. If your employer contributes to a fund, you won't get a separate gratuity payment at the end.

## Why your settlement might be lower than you expected

Your final settlement isn't always the gratuity figure you calculated. Your employer can deduct money you owe them, such as an unpaid salary advance. Unpaid leave reduces your service, as in Example 4. And if HR uses the ×12÷365 method, the figure comes out slightly lower.

Your bank can also get there first. If you have a personal loan or credit card tied to salary transfer, the bank may have asked your employer for a letter agreeing to send your final settlement to the bank. Your gratuity then goes towards the loan before it reaches you.

This catches many people out. If you have a salary-transfer loan, call your bank before your last day and ask how much they'll take from your settlement. If you also have card debt, our guide to [credit card statement cycles](/blog/credit-card-statement-cycle-uae) explains how to stop interest building while you sort it out.

If your employer doesn't pay within 14 days, you can file a complaint with MOHRE through its app, website, or by calling 80084.

## What to do with your gratuity

For many people in the UAE, gratuity is the largest single payment they'll ever get from an employer. It goes further if you use it in this order:

1. Pay off expensive debt. Card balances at 3% a month cost more than any savings account earns.
2. Top up your emergency fund. If you're between jobs, this is the money that buys you time.
3. Cover the next big bill. If a rent cheque or school term is coming up, move that amount into a [sinking fund](/blog/sinking-funds-uae-rent-cheques-school-fees) before it gets spent.

Until the money is in your account, don't plan around it. Settlements can be delayed or disputed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is gratuity calculated on basic or gross salary?

Basic salary only. Housing, transport and other allowances aren't included.

### Do I get gratuity if I resign before one year?

No. You need at least one year of continuous service. After that, resigning doesn't reduce it.

### Is there an official gratuity calculator in the UAE?

Some government bodies, such as the Dubai Development Authority, offer online gratuity calculators, and many news and HR sites do too. Most use the ÷30 method. The four steps above give you the same result, and you can check each number.

### Is gratuity taxed in the UAE?

No. The UAE doesn't tax personal income, and gratuity is no exception. If you're moving to another country, check whether it taxes money you bring in.

### What happens to gratuity if I'm on probation?

Probation generally counts towards your service if you stay on after it. If you leave before completing a year in total, there's no gratuity.

## Check your number before your last day

Take your latest payslip and find your basic salary. Work out your service, take off any unpaid leave, and run the four steps. Then ask HR for their figure in writing and compare the two.

If they don't match, the reason is usually one of the ones above, and it's much easier to sort out before you leave than after.
