# Rent Cheques in Dubai: 1, 2, 4 or 12, Which Saves You More?

The same flat in Dubai can cost you a different amount depending on how many cheques you write. Landlords often knock a few percent off for one cheque and add a few percent for twelve. On a AED 100,000 flat, that gap can reach AED 10,000 a year.

Rent cheques in Dubai are usually post-dated and handed over when you sign the lease, so the choice locks you in for the year. This guide works through the real yearly cost of 1, 2, 4 and 12 cheques on the same flat, what the new monthly rent platforms charge, and how to move to fewer cheques without borrowing.

All prices here are examples. Discounts and fees vary by landlord, area and year, so ask for each option in writing before you sign.

## How many cheques for rent in Dubai?

There's no fixed rule. The number is agreed between you and the landlord, and the common options are:

- 1 cheque: the full year's rent upfront
- 2 cheques: every six months
- 4 cheques: every three months
- 6 cheques: every two months
- 12 cheques: monthly

Four cheques is a common middle ground and is often the price quoted in listings. When demand is high, landlords push for fewer cheques. When the market cools, they accept more.

### Move-in costs on top of the rent cheques

The rent cheques aren't the only money you need on signing day. Budget for these as well:

- A security deposit, usually paid by separate cheque. The standard in Dubai is 5% of the annual rent for an unfurnished home and 10% for a furnished one, though it can be negotiated. You get it back at the end of the lease, minus any damage.
- An agency fee if you rent through a broker, commonly 5% of the annual rent plus VAT.
- Ejari registration, which costs roughly AED 100 to 250 depending on where you register.
- A DEWA deposit for electricity and water, typically around AED 2,000 for an apartment and more for a villa.
- Moving costs, from a van and movers to new furniture.

On the AED 95,000 single-cheque price in the example below, a 5% deposit, 5% agency fee with VAT, Ejari and a DEWA deposit add up to about AED 12,000. That means close to AED 107,000 leaves your account in the week you move in. If you're stretching to pay one cheque, check you can cover these too.

## Rent cheques in Dubai: 1 vs 4 vs 12, the real cost

Landlords price by cheque count because fewer cheques mean less risk and the money arrives sooner. Recent reporting puts the single-cheque discount at roughly 3% to 7% off the four-cheque price, and the premium for twelve cheques at about 3% to 5%.

Here's how that looks on a flat listed at AED 100,000 a year on four cheques. The discounts and premiums are typical figures, not quotes.

| Cheques | Example adjustment | Yearly rent | Each payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5% discount | AED 95,000 | AED 95,000 once |
| 2 | 2.5% discount | AED 97,500 | AED 48,750 every 6 months |
| 4 | Listed price | AED 100,000 | AED 25,000 every 3 months |
| 12 | 5% premium | AED 105,000 | AED 8,750 a month |

The difference between one cheque and twelve is AED 10,000 in this example. That's more than a month's rent.

### Is the discount worth paying upfront?

Paying upfront has a cost too. Money that leaves your account in month one can't earn interest for the rest of the year.

Compare 1 cheque with 4. With four cheques, you hold on to some of the money for longer: AED 75,000 for the first three months, AED 50,000 for the next three, and AED 25,000 for three more. That averages out at about AED 37,500 over the year.

If your savings earn 4% a year, that's worth about AED 1,500. The single-cheque discount in the example is AED 5,000. So if you already have the money saved, one cheque usually wins by a clear margin.

Use your own savings rate. The lower it is, the more one cheque comes out ahead.

The same pattern holds on a smaller home. Take a studio listed at AED 60,000 on four cheques. With the same 5% discount and 5% premium, one cheque costs AED 57,000 and twelve cost AED 63,000, a gap of AED 6,000. Four cheques would let you keep about AED 22,500 in savings on average through the year, which earns around AED 900 at 4%. One cheque still saves about AED 3,000 against four, as long as the money is already sitting in your account.

### When one cheque costs you more

The maths changes completely if you borrow to pay it.

Credit card balances in the UAE usually cost around 3% a month. Putting AED 95,000 on a card and paying it off over the year would cost far more in interest than the AED 5,000 you saved. A personal loan is cheaper than a card, but the interest and processing fee can still wipe out the discount.

Some landlords accept card payments, and some banks offer installment plans on rent. These come with fees too. If you pay by card, our guide to [credit card statement cycles](/blog/credit-card-statement-cycle-uae) explains how to avoid interest.

One cheque also ties up cash. If paying it would empty your savings, you'd have nothing left for a job loss or medical bill. Keep your [emergency fund](/blog/emergency-fund-uae) separate and only pay one cheque from money above that.

## 12 cheques and monthly rent payments in Dubai

Monthly rent is getting easier to arrange, and there are three main ways to do it.

Start by speaking with your landlord. Some accept 12 monthly cheques directly, usually with a premium of a few percent.

If the landlord won't, a rent payment platform can fill the gap. Several companies now pay your landlord upfront and let you pay them monthly, often by card. The landlord gets their usual cheques, and you pay a fee for the convenience. Fee tables published in 2026 show:

- Property Finder's Rent in 12: about 11.8% if the landlord would normally want 1 cheque, and 8% if they'd want 2
- Engel & Völkers' monthly option: from about 8% when the landlord wants 2 payments down to 4.75% when they accept 6

These are fees on top of the rent. On our AED 95,000 single-cheque price, an 11.8% fee adds about AED 11,210, so you'd pay around AED 106,210 a year, or AED 8,851 a month. That's more than asking the landlord for 12 cheques directly.

The third route is government schemes. The Dubai Land Department has announced initiatives aimed at making monthly payments easier. The details and launch dates have changed more than once, so check the [DLD's website](https://dubailand.gov.ae) for what's available when you sign.

Monthly payments can still make sense. If the choice is between 12 payments and borrowing on a credit card to pay one cheque, a fee of 5% to 8% is usually cheaper than a year of card interest. Compare the total yearly cost, not the monthly amount.

## What happens if a rent cheque bounces?

Writing post-dated cheques for a whole year means planning for a whole year. If your salary is late or you lose your job, a cheque can bounce.

Since 2 January 2022, a bounced cheque for insufficient funds is no longer a crime in the UAE, unless it was written in bad faith. That doesn't mean there are no consequences:

- The landlord can take a civil case to recover the money, and can ask the court to enforce the cheque directly.
- Under Dubai's tenancy law, the landlord can seek eviction if you don't pay within 30 days of a formal notice.
- The bounced cheque is reported to the Al Etihad Credit Bureau, which can make future loans and cards harder to get.
- Your bank will charge a fee for the returned cheque.

More cheques mean more chances for one to bounce, but each one is smaller. Fewer cheques mean each one is large. Pick the number your cash flow can handle on a bad month, not a good one.

If you can see a cheque won't clear, talk to your landlord before the date. Many will agree to hold it for a few days or replace it, which is far easier than dealing with a returned cheque.

## How to move to fewer cheques without borrowing

Most people can't jump from four cheques to one in a single renewal. You'd need to pay this year's rent and save the whole of next year's at the same time.

It's easier in steps:

1. Each month after payday, move one twelfth of your yearly rent into a separate account. For AED 100,000, that's about AED 8,333, and each cheque then clears from money that's already set aside. Our guide to [sinking funds for rent cheques and school fees](/blog/sinking-funds-uae-rent-cheques-school-fees) explains how to set this up.
2. Put a bit more aside each month than the cheques need. The extra builds up into a buffer for the next renewal.
3. Add windfalls to the buffer. Annual bonuses, a ticket allowance, or [gratuity from a previous job](/blog/gratuity-calculation-uae) can take you most of the way to the bigger first payment.
4. Step down at renewal: from 4 cheques to 2 at your next renewal, then from 2 to 1 the year after, once the buffer is big enough.

## Negotiating your rent cheques

The cheque count is part of the price, so treat it like one.

Ask the landlord or agent for the yearly price at each cheque count you're considering, and get it in writing. Compare the yearly totals, not the size of each cheque.

Ask about the dates as well as the number. A cheque dated just after payday is far less likely to bounce than one dated just before it.

At renewal, use the cheque count as a bargaining point. Offering fewer cheques can sometimes keep the rent flat in a year when it would otherwise go up.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it cheaper to pay rent in one cheque in Dubai?

Usually, yes. Landlords often give a discount of around 3% to 7% for one cheque compared with four. It's only cheaper overall if you pay from savings, not from a loan or credit card.

### Can I pay rent monthly in Dubai?

Yes, if your landlord agrees to 12 cheques or you use a rent payment platform. Expect to pay a premium or fee for the convenience, often somewhere between about 4% and 12% depending on the option.

### Do I need a separate cheque for the security deposit?

Usually, yes. The deposit is normally 5% of the annual rent for an unfurnished home and 10% for a furnished one, paid separately and refunded at the end of the lease, minus any damage.

### Can I change the number of cheques when I renew?

Yes. The number of cheques is agreed for each lease, so you can negotiate a different number at renewal.

## Pick the number you can always cover

The cheapest option on paper is one cheque. The cheapest option for you is the fewest cheques you can pay from savings without touching your emergency fund or borrowing.

Before your next lease or renewal, ask for the yearly price at each cheque count, check it against what you've saved, and set up a rent fund so next year's choice is easier.
